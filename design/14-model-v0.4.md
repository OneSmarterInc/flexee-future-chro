# The Future CHRO: pipeline model v0.4

26 Nov 2026. Adds decision FF-D1 to model v0.3: Ruth Adeyemi, a senior tax partner, asks to retire early in 2032. All 29 named checks pass (27 from v0.3 and 2 new). The PHP engine and the prototype engine reproduce every fixture value.

## What changed from v0.3

| Mechanism | How the engine carries it | New data |
|---|---|---|
| Ruth's terms (2032) | A 2032 choice of three: let her go in June (the default), keep her two years on judgment and teaching, or run a one-year capture project. She leaves at the start of 2032, at the end of 2032 or at the end of 2033. When she goes, 6 points of firm knowledge leave with her. The share kept is the usual share for where the firm keeps its knowledge, or 70% after a capture year, or 50% after two years of teaching. Her clients' fees, $9 million a year, drop by 25%, 10% or 5% from the year she leaves. Keeping her costs $0.9 million a year and adds mentoring; the capture project costs $0.6 million | 11 constants |

A team that never decides on Ruth lets her go. That's every team in the slice, and the four reference teams, so their numbers move a little from 2032.

## The four reference teams

The order and every lesson stay the same.

## The two new checks

1. Letting Ruth go costs the most of her three terms: profit from 2032 to 2035 is lowest with "go" for every team in every future. Keeping her is worth $8 million to $17 million over those four years.
2. A capture project keeps her knowledge where the firm hasn't already. By 2035, the middle team holds 3 more points of firm knowledge with capture than without. The builder, which already keeps its knowledge in a firm-owned system, keeps the same either way.

## Limits

The 11 new constants are assumptions. Ruth's terms move the overall result by under one point, which is right for one person in a firm of 1,700. The lesson is in the story and in the fees, not in the judgment.
