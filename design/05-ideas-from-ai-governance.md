# The Future CHRO: ideas from Flexee AI Strategic Governance

25 Nov 2026. Read from Vikram's Drive folder Flexee/Flexee-AI: the program one-pager, the AI Decision Economics design sketch, and handbook sections 3 (design rationale) and 11 (consequence threading). The governance program is a five-day in-person executive program. Teams of five (CEO, CFO, CIO, General Counsel, CHRO) run Meridian Industrial Components through six rounds of AI-generated analyses with planted flaws. It is grounded in the Sethi and Jayaraj verification economics paper.

## The big overlap

Our hidden review work (model v0.2) is verification economics seen from the people side. The governance program asks how much to check each AI output. The Future CHRO asks who does the checking, what it costs them, and what happens to the pipeline of people who can check. The two fit together. The paper can give Future CHRO the academic grounding that the closest competitor (Future Work Academy) doesn't have.

## Ideas to carry over

| Idea in the governance program | How it would work in Future CHRO | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Three task types: Type 1 is cheap to check, Type 2 can be checked but takes time, Type 3 can't be checked without redoing the work | Give each workflow a type. Audit testing and billing are Type 2, advisory research is Type 3, scheduling is Type 1. Leaving Type 3 work to AI alone gives errors that nobody catches until a client does | Model (`workflows.csv`), workflow card |
| The verification wall: in round 5 the load of checks exceeds what the team can do | Make our 2031 pipeline gap the wall. AI output keeps growing while the senior people who check it are fewer. When review demand passes senior capacity, errors and strain jump together | Model (review capacity), week 5 |
| Calibrated artifacts: pre-written AI analyses with one planted flaw each | Each year the team gets one AI-written HR analysis: a workforce plan that leaves out review hours, an attrition forecast, a candidate assessment, a skills map. The team spends its own limited checking time on it. Participants feel verification economics while they manage it in the firm | New element in each year, pre-written and the same for every class |
| Consequence threads: named links from an early decision to a later event, with a facilitator reference | Write down our threads: the 2027 graduate offers lead to the 2031 shortage of seniors; the 2029 knowledge choice leads to the 2031 vendor terms; 2028 strain leads to the 2032 early retirement; 2027 checking levels lead to the 2030 outage | Faculty guide, threads reference |
| Spend on checking capacity rather than better AI | A new choice: train reviewers and build checking tools, which lowers the review rate. The model then shows that this pays better than buying faster AI | Model, Head of AI and Workforce Governance card |
| Fixed roles, played outside your home function | Seat people away from their day job where the class allows (an HR leader takes the AI governance seat) | Facilitator guide |
| Name the framework late | Let teams live through the hidden review hours in weeks 1 to 4. Name verification economics in the week 5 debrief, and give the full framework at the end | Facilitator guide |
| Score against the best choice, not a rubric | Our judgment score already does this across three futures. Add one line per year in the debrief: the verification level the paper's model would pick, against the team's choice | Debrief |
| Buyers: board education, NACD, Conference Board; licensing to Wharton and IMD; commercial work through Chuck | Same buyers for the executive format. Future CHRO can be sold as the people half of the governance program | Go-to-market |

## What the governance program does that we should not copy

It runs on paper and conversation with no software engine. Our engine decides results, and teams can replay them under three futures. It is single-future and its traps are fixed. Ours has three hidden futures, so the same choice can be right or wrong depending on what comes.
