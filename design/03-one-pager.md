# Flexee: The Future CHRO
## Leadership and human capital in the age of AI

Your team is the people leadership of Ashby Calder, an audit, tax and advisory firm of about 2,000 people. The years run from 2027 to 2035. AI gets better every year, but nobody knows how fast, how reliable or how regulated it will be. The Managing Partner wants growth, the CFO wants lower cost, the CIO wants to move fast, and your people want a future. Each simulated year you decide how work is split between people and AI, how you keep growing experts and leaders, how you treat and pay people, and how you govern the AI. Some choices pay at once and cost you years later.

### Who it's for

Executive education, EMBA programs, consulting firms and corporate HR teams. Teams of three to five sit as the HR leadership team: Chief HR Officer, Head of Talent, Head of Learning, and Head of AI and Workforce Governance. Each seat owns its own decisions, and the team agrees on the year together. It runs as a one- or two-day workshop of eight years, or as a term with one year a week. Both formats use one engine.

### How it works

A tested numbers model decides every result. It tracks people moving from analyst to partner, the work AI can take on each year, the review hours that checking AI quietly consumes, strain on the people doing the checking, the firm's knowledge and who holds it, trust, and the CHRO's standing with the CIO and the board. AI gives voice to the people at the table and writes the stories of each year, using only the numbers the model provides.

One of three futures is drawn for each class and kept hidden: rapid autonomy, AI as a complement to people, or a constrained world of errors, regulation and resistance. The signals each year point in different directions. At the debrief, each team's decisions are replayed under all three futures. A plan that holds up in all three shows judgment. A plan that wins only in the future that arrived shows luck. Teams see both.

### What participants learn

| Skill | Where they practise it |
|---|---|
| Designing work at the level of workflows | For each workflow, choosing who does the work: people, people with AI help, AI with people checking, or AI alone |
| Keeping the pipeline of experts and leaders | Graduate intake, mentoring, and AI that coaches people rather than replaces their practice |
| Seeing the hidden costs of AI | Review hours that savings figures leave out, and the strain they put on senior staff |
| Governing AI at work | How much AI may do unchecked, and what an error or an outage costs |
| Keeping what the firm knows | Capturing expert knowledge, and the risk of leaving it with a vendor |
| Leading change people can trust | Layoffs, sharing the gains, and telling people early what will change |
| Earning a place at the top table | Standing with the CFO, the CIO and the board, and who plans the work of AI |
| Deciding under deep uncertainty | Reading mixed signals, and building plans that work across futures |

### What makes it different

The business schools that run CHRO and AI programs teach with lectures and analytics. The large simulation publishers sell HR simulations about staffing, pay and engagement, and leadership simulations about teams and change. None of the products we found deals with AI taking over work. One new graduate-course simulation puts a single student in a manufacturer's CEO seat for eight weeks of AI decisions, graded by AI. Ours is built for executives and HR leadership teams. A tested model decides every result, and every team's plan is replayed under three futures. The core problem has been in the news all through 2026: AI takes over entry-level work, and the future supply of experts and leaders shrinks. Here, teams feel that problem for themselves, five simulated years after they made the choice that caused it.
