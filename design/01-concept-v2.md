# Flexee: The Future CHRO — Concept v2

Prepared 23 Nov 2026 from Vikram's concept note of November 2026 and decisions H1 to H6 (`claude/future-chro-decision-register.md`). It replaces the paused "Leading People in the Age of AI" (`claude/leadership-hr-ai-status.md`).

## 1. The sim in a paragraph

Your team is the HR leadership of a successful company of about 2,000 people, from 2027 to 2035. AI is getting better every year, but nobody knows how fast, how reliable or how regulated it will be. The CEO wants growth, the CFO wants lower cost, the CIO wants to move fast, and the people want a future. Each simulated year you decide how work is split between people and AI agents, how you keep building experts and leaders, how you pay and treat people, and how you govern the AI. Some choices pay at once and cost years later. At the end you see what you built, and how your same decisions would have played out in the two futures that didn't happen.

## 2. Market and formats (H1, H2)

| Format | Who | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Workshop (built first)** | Executive education, EMBA, consulting firms, corporate HR teams | 1 or 2 days. 8 rounds of about 45 minutes, one simulated year each, with a debrief |
| Term | EMBA and MBA electives | One round a week, with readings and a written reflection |

One engine serves both. An MBA version for the MBA core (MBA 7500) can follow.

## 3. Who plays (H3)

Teams of three to five act as the HR leadership team. Each seat owns some decisions and has its own conversations, and the team decides together.

| Seat | Owns |
|---|---|
| Chief HR Officer | Strategy, the executive table, compensation, organization design |
| Head of Talent | Hiring, the workforce mix, contractors, retention |
| Head of Learning | Apprenticeships, mentoring, leadership development |
| Head of AI and Workforce Governance | Where AI may act alone, oversight, risk, regulation |

A team of three merges Talent and Learning. A team of five adds an Employee Relations seat.

## 4. The engine: expertise as an asset

The core of the engine is a pipeline of expertise. People move up only by doing the work, with someone teaching them.

| Level | Becomes the next level through |
|---|---|
| Entry | Years of real tasks, with feedback |
| Experienced | Harder work, mentoring, a chance to lead |
| Expert | Judgment built on cases, and teaching others |
| Leader | Picked from experts and experienced people |

**What the student moves.** How many people join at entry level, how much entry work goes to AI, whether AI is used as a tutor that speeds learning or as a substitute that removes it, how much mentoring time experts give, pay and promotion rules, and how far AI may act without a person.

**What moves on its own.** AI capability grows by the hidden future (section 6). People leave when trust and prospects fall, and experts retire. Expertise wears away when it isn't used.

**The core trade-off the engine carries.** Automating entry work lowers cost in the first two or three years. It also shrinks the flow into the pipeline, and the shortage of experts and leaders shows five or six years later. AI used as a tutor costs more at first and builds a stronger pipeline. Whether either pays off depends on the future that arrives.

**The numbers come first.** A Python model with golden fixtures, as in Halden, before any screen. Reference teams: careful, average, careless, run under all three futures.

## 5. One round

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. The year in brief | Results, what changed in AI, the market and the law, signals about which future is coming |
| 2. The table | Conversations with AI stakeholders: CEO, CFO, CIO, the board chair, the employee council, and one named person this year. They remember what you said before |
| 3. Decide | Three or four big decisions, each owned by a seat, from the eight domains in the concept note |
| 4. One moment | A named case that makes the strategy real, for example a senior expert who wants to retire early, the graduate class you didn't hire, a team leader replaced by an AI agent |
| 5. Results | Numbers, a short story of the year, and what your people say |
| 6. Revise | The team writes one paragraph: what it now believes about the future, and what it will change |

The engine decides every result. AI voices the stakeholders and the stories, using only what the engine gives it.

## 6. Three futures (H4)

| Future | What happens to AI |
|---|---|
| Rapid autonomy | Agents do complex professional and managerial work by about 2031 |
| Complementarity | AI raises human output a lot, but human judgment stays central |
| Constrained | Reliability problems, regulation and resistance slow adoption |

One future is drawn for each class and stays hidden. Each round gives signals, some of them misleading. At the debrief the engine replays each team's exact decisions under all three futures. A strategy that holds up in all three shows judgment. One that wins only in the future that happened shows luck. This is how the sim separates judgment from outcome, as the concept note asks.

## 7. How teams are measured

| Measure | Examples |
|---|---|
| Financial performance | Revenue, cost, productivity |
| Expertise and succession | Experts and ready leaders at each level, the pipeline five years out |
| Trust and health | Trust in leadership, attrition, the employee council's view |
| AI maturity and governance | Oversight in place, incidents, compliance |
| Resilience | How the company does when an AI system fails or a vendor changes terms |
| Influence | The CHRO's standing with the CEO, CFO, CIO and board |
| Judgment | How well the decisions hold up across all three futures, and the quality of the yearly paragraph |

A playable slice of three rounds: 2027, 2029 and 2031.

## 10. Market scan (23 Nov 2026)

- **Executive programs teach this, without a simulation of this kind.** Wharton, Cambridge, Berkeley, Chicago Booth and INSEAD run CHRO or human-capital-and-AI programs. Their published descriptions show lectures, analytics and practical exercises. The scan found none that names a multi-year simulation.
- **Workforce simulation tools exist for planning, not teaching.** Vendors such as Gloat offer scenario simulators for real workforce plans.
- **One close student project.** An MIT Sloan student project with Accenture built a simulation called "The Future of Work: Navigating Workforce Dynamics in the Age of GenAI".
- **The core mechanism is a live topic.** McKinsey, PwC, the World Economic Forum and IMD all published in 2026 on AI shrinking entry-level work and the risk to the future leadership pipeline.

The scan used public web pages only. A deeper check of simulation publishers (Harvard Business Publishing, Forio, Cesim, Capsim) comes before any market claim.
